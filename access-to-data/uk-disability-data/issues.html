Disability issues in the UK

Start with the issue. Then look at the evidence.

Each topic is designed to separate what the data shows from possible explanations, different perspectives and the limits of the available evidence.

Natural and fair

No issue is reduced to one number.

Disabled people are not one group. Experiences vary by impairment, age, income, geography and many other factors. This site keeps definitions visible and avoids turning association into cause.

Evidence framework

Every detailed issue page should answer the same five questions.

1. What does the data show?Headline figures, trends and comparisons.
2. Why does it matter?A short plain-English explanation without campaigning language.
3. What might explain it?Evidence-based factors, clearly attributed where interpretation is involved.
4. What do different sources say?Government, official statistics and research kept identifiable.
5. What can’t the data tell us?Missing groups, uncertainty, definition changes and limits on causal claims.

Evidence labels

Know what kind of evidence you are looking at.

The site will use visible evidence-type labels so a survey estimate is never made to look identical to a census count, administrative caseload or research finding.

Official statistic / surveyEstimate or published analysis from an official statistical source.
CensusCount based on census questions and definitions.
Administrative dataRecords generated through a public service or benefit system.
Research findingA finding attributed to the research organisation or study that produced it.
