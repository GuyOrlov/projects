Read the numbers in context

One number never tells the whole story.

Disabled people have different experiences. Age, disability or impairment, income, where people live and other factors can affect the figures. A link between two things does not prove that one caused the other.

How we explain each issue

For every issue, we ask five simple questions.

1. What do the numbers show?The main figures, trends and comparisons.
2. Why does it matter?A short explanation in plain English.
3. What might explain it?Possible factors supported by evidence, clearly linked to the source.
4. What do different sources say?We keep government data, official statistics and research clearly labelled.
5. What can’t the data tell us?Who may be missing, what is uncertain and what the figures cannot prove.

How to read the evidence

Not all figures are the same kind of data.

We label each figure so you can see whether it comes from a survey, a census, public-service records or research.

Official statistics or surveyFigures published by an official statistics body.
CensusA population count based on census questions and definitions.
Public-service recordsFigures from a benefit, service or other public system.
ResearchA finding from a named study or research organisation.
